What this is

DOWNRANGE is a personal shot-group precision analyzer. Photograph a target (or use a blank reference grid), mark your shots and point of aim, and it computes the same statistics ballistic-analysis software gives you — extreme spread, mean radius, CEP, standard deviations, MOA/mil conversions — plus load-development tools most tools don't have: composite groups that show how each load deviates from your zero, charge and seating-depth ladder charts, environmental logging, and session-wide zero checking. Everything is stored locally in your browser — nothing is uploaded anywhere.

Quick start

  1. On the Setup tab, load a target photo, or click Blank Grid if you don't have one.
  2. Switch to Calibrate mode, click two points a known distance apart (a grid square, ruler mark, bull diameter), and enter that distance when the field appears.
  3. Switch to Aim Point mode and click the bullseye / zero point.
  4. Switch to Shot mode and click each bullet hole. Click an existing marker to remove it.
  5. Fill in whatever metadata matters to you — name, distance, caliber, ammo, charge, seating depth, velocities, conditions, notes, tags.
  6. Click Save Group. The photo and calibration stay loaded, so if there's another bullseye on the same sheet, hit New Target (or just re-mark the aim point) and mark it next.
  7. Switch to the Analysis tab to review, compare, combine, or visualize your saved groups.

Feature reference

Calibration & units

Calibration converts pixels to real-world units. Pick millimeters, centimeters, or inches before calibrating — recalibrating overwrites the scale for the current photo. Set your usual defaults on the Settings tab.

Marking: shots, aim point, cold-bore

Shot mode adds/removes bullet holes. Aim Point marks your point of aim (needed for windage/elevation/zero-shift numbers). Cold-Bore marks a single shot — typically your first round of the string — shown as a distinct diamond marker and reported separately; it is never included in the group's own statistics.

Multiple groups per photo

Saving a group keeps the photo, calibration, zoom, and distance loaded. For a sheet with several bullseyes, just click New Target (or re-mark Aim Point directly) to clear the current unsaved shots/aim and mark the next one.

Zoom, pan & pinch

Scroll wheel zooms toward the cursor. Two-finger pinch zooms on touch devices. Switch to Pan mode to drag the view. Markers stay a constant on-screen size regardless of zoom, so precision doesn't degrade when you zoom in.

Caliber & to-scale holes

Pick a bullet caliber and shot markers render at true diameter (scaled through calibration and zoom), and the table adds a "max spread, edge-to-edge" row — the classic caliper-measurement convention (center-to-center + one bullet diameter).

Velocity & conditions

Enter space- or comma-separated velocities per group; average, extreme spread, and SD are computed automatically. Optional temperature, humidity, and wind fields let you log conditions that can confound a load-development comparison across sessions.

Small-sample warning

Groups with fewer than 5 shots show a note reminding you that extreme spread and CEP get noisy at small sample sizes — worth keeping in mind before reading too much into a 3-shot group.

Tags & filtering

Add comma-separated tags to any group. The Saved Groups filter box matches against name or tags.

Editing a saved group

Click Edit on any group card — it jumps you to the Setup tab and reconstructs the group's shots and aim point on a reference grid. Add, remove, or nudge shots exactly like a live session, edit any field, then Update to save changes back into that same group, or Cancel to discard.

Composite groups

Select two or more saved groups and combine them. If every member has a marked point of aim, the composite centers on point of aim — each source group's own mean point of impact draws as a large hollow circle in that load's color (small dots are individual shots), kept visually distinct from the blue point-of-aim crosshair and the brass crosshair marking the overall pooled center, so you can see exactly how each load deviates from POA at a glance. A per-load breakdown table lists each source's offset, ES, and MR side by side. If any member lacks a point of aim, it falls back to centering each group on its own mean impact point instead (pooled precision only, no accuracy claim).

Group Summary

Pick any group and see it plotted visually — on the original photo (if attached when saving) or an auto-fit reference grid. Export as PNG to share.

Compare Groups

Every group's key numbers side by side, including Dispersion, with its own unit selector — mm/cm/in for linear, or mrad/MOA for angular (using each group's own distance; groups without a distance show "—" in angular mode). Tightest and loosest groups are highlighted automatically.

Zero Check

Pools windage/elevation across every single group with both a point of aim and a distance — a quick check on whether your zero is holding steady across sessions. Each group has an "Include in Zero Check" toggle (on by default) so load-development groups — shot to compare charges or seating depths, not to confirm your zero — don't skew the pooled result. Excluded groups show a "NOT ZEROED" badge in the group list. View results in MOA, MRAD, or a linear unit (mm/cm/in, converted at a distance you set) — same unit options used elsewhere in the app.

Scope offset: if you've already dialed a known correction into your scope to preserve your point of aim — rather than re-zeroing — enter that correction (MOA windage/elevation) under "Apply scope offset." Zero Check will then report drift relative to that adjusted baseline instead of raw zero, so it reflects where your rifle actually sits right now.

Trend & ladder charts

Plots a chosen metric — group size stats, Dispersion, or velocity average/SD — against your choice of x-axis: save order (chronological), Charge, or Seating depth. Charge and Seating modes only include groups where that field is set, order by it, and label each point with its value — the classic ladder/OCW-test chart for spotting a flat "node" while developing a load. Units are shown directly on the chart axis and can be set independently of your marking unit — mm/cm/in, or mrad/MOA (using each group's own distance; groups lacking one are excluded from that view rather than shown wrong).

Velocity box plot: tick "Show velocity as box plot" to replace the line chart with one box per group showing the full min/median/max spread of that group's velocity readings — much more informative than a single average point for comparing consistency across charges or loads. Still respects the load/tag filter and x-axis mode.

Dispersion

The average of a group's horizontal and vertical standard deviations — a single-number summary of spread, shown with extra decimal precision since it's a derived average. Available in the per-group stats table, the Compare Groups table, and as a Trend metric.

Export & import

Points CSV and Summary CSV give you raw and computed data. Export Session saves everything — groups, metadata, attached photos — as one JSON file; Import Session restores it, here or on another device.

Settings

Set your default measurement, distance, and velocity units so new groups start with what you normally use.

Calibration

A separate tool for exterior-ballistics truing — not group geometry. Build a Ballistic Profile for a rifle+load (MV, BC, drag model, zero distance, sight height), then compare a long-range group against what the profile predicts. If there's a mismatch, solve for a corrected MV or BC — from one group, or from several at different distances for a combined fit — and generate a Dope Card from the result. This uses a simplified point-mass model (no spin drift, Coriolis, or aerodynamic jump) built from public-domain drag data and independently checked against published trajectory figures — treat it as a solid starting point, not a replacement for verifying your own dope on paper.

Tips

  • Distance stays filled in after each save, since it's usually constant across every group at one session.
  • Zero-shift and MOA/mil figures need a distance set on that group to appear.
  • CEP is shown two ways: measured (median distance from center across actual shots) and calculated (approximation from standard deviations) — they can diverge on small groups, which is normal.
  • Log charge weight and/or seating depth on every group during a ladder test to unlock the Trend chart's ladder view.

Data & privacy

Everything is stored locally in this browser only. Nothing is sent anywhere. Export a session backup for a durable copy.
